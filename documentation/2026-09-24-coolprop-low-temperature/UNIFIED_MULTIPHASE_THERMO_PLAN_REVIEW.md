# Unified multiphase thermodynamics plan: review and route investigation

Status: Concluded 2026-09-24 (review of `UNIFIED_MULTIPHASE_THERMO_PLAN.md` revision 2, with an independent investigation of the implementation route and of the data sources). No product source, data record, test or dependency was changed.

Outcome (2026-09-24): the owner accepted the recommended decisions of section 7 ("Use recommended decisions"). They are recorded as D1 to D5 in [DECISION_LOG.md](DECISION_LOG.md), and the plan was raised to revision 3 with the amendments of section 6. D5 (the VDU K-value policy) has no recommendation here and is settled in P0.

Inputs: the plan and its six supporting reviews; the code at main `f9d6be1` (0.4.0); a reference probe of the current PR78 formulation against CoolProp 8.0.0 (`research/2026-09-24-coolprop-low-temperature/pr78-vs-coolprop/`); a comparison of the bundled ideal-gas Cp fits with NIST JANAF values (same folder); four web-research reports (NeqSim/DWSIM reuse; open reference-EOS, ideal-gas and IAPWS sources; cubic-EOS route, hydrogen data and process anchors; solid-phase and SLE/SVE data), run on opus subagents on 2026-09-24. Facts the agents could not open at the source are marked as such in section 7. Owner statement during the review: the project is non-commercial use, so non-commercial data licences are acceptable.

## 1. Verdict

The plan's gating (a built and integrated four-compound pilot before any broad compound rollout, process-indexed before/target/achieved domain tables, explicit unsupported results) is sound and should stay. Its weakness is that it postpones the two decisions that determine everything else, the fluid model and the data spine, to an open-ended P1 "compare a small number of credible options" study. The measurements and sources gathered here are enough to close most of that study now:

1. **Route: extend the in-house cubic, not adopt a library.** The current PR78 with its constant translation is already within about 1.5 % on saturated liquid density and about 1 % on saturation pressure from the triple points of N2, CH4, C2H6 and CO2 up to reduced temperatures of about 0.85, within 0.6 % on hydrogen density at hydrotreating conditions, and within 5 % on dense supercritical density outside the critical neighbourhood (section 3). That is "estimated with declared error" grade, which is one of the plan's own capability states, everywhere except the critical neighbourhood and liquid heat capacity near it. The reference Helmholtz equations do not buy the plan's high-temperature target: CoolProp's methane and ethane equations end at 625 K and 675 K, ethylene at 450 K, and at steam-cracking conditions the residual part of the enthalpy is below 0.1 % anyway, so the 1073 to 1143 K target is purely an ideal-gas Cp problem. NeqSim is a 60 MB fat jar with unrelocated log4j, gson and commons-lang3 and an H2 database loaded from CSV, with open issues on placeholder vapour-pressure data; it is an offline oracle, not a runtime dependency. Section 4 gives the comparison.
2. **Two defects in the current formulation must go before any pressure extension, and the plan should say so as prerequisites rather than as one P3 bullet.** The network's liquid path (translated PR at a fixed 2 MPa reference, carried to the state pressure by a global compressibility of 1e-9 /Pa) has no liquid reference state wherever the saturation pressure exceeds 2 MPa, which is every fluid above roughly 0.9 Tc; the measured error there is −20 % to −95 % in density. The degree-five ideal-gas Cp fits diverge above 900 K: methane is −5.9 % at 1143 K and −10.8 % at 1200 K.
3. **Data spine.** One shared ideal-gas reference (NASA CEA 9-coefficient polynomials, Apache-2.0, with ATcT formation enthalpies as already carried in CoolProp's fluid files) under every evaluator is what makes cross-package enthalpies consistent and later reaction enthalpies possible. The plan asks for that consistency in words; it should name the mechanism in P2.
4. **Solids.** Make CO2 the single solid-forming pilot species, qualified for both deposition in nitrogen (P4) and freezing out of liquid methane (P5), because a consistent solid CO2 Gibbs equation exists (Jäger and Span 2012), the experimental frost-point and solid-liquid data are the richest of any candidate, and the same crystal serves both transitions. Keep the methane/ethane liquidus as a research-only case: ethane has a solid-solid transition 0.55 K below its triple point with four times its fusion enthalpy, and the eutectic at 73.88 K lies 17 K below both pure triple points, where every fluid model is an extrapolation.
5. **Sourcing.** Everything the pilot needs is available under open terms and is listed by data class in section 5, with the independent holdouts in section 6.

## 2. Findings on the plan

Severity: **must** (blocks the pilot or makes a stated acceptance claim unreachable), **should** (changes the route or the evidence), **note**.

| # | Severity | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | must | **The 2 MPa liquid reference path fails structurally above Psat = 2 MPa.** `HydrocarbonModel.phase` evaluates every liquid at `REFERENCE_PRESSURE = 2e6` and carries it to the state pressure with `GlobalLiquidResponse` (k = 1e-9 /Pa). Where Psat(T) > 2 MPa (nitrogen above about 115 K, methane above 165 K, ethane above 270 K, CO2 above 253 K) the reference state is a vapour root; the probe measures −22 % (N2 at 120 K), −90 % (CH4 at 185 K), −92 % (C2H6 at 300 K / 10 MPa) and −95 % (CO2 at 290 K) in liquid density. Inside the current 2 MPa ceiling this cannot be reached, which is why nothing has failed so far; the plan's 10 MPa target reaches it for every pilot fluid. The global k is also 2 to 9 times smaller than the real isothermal compressibility of the cryogenic liquids (N2: 3.2e-9 at 77 K, 9.3e-9 at 100 K; CH4: 2.2e-9 at 112 K), so even where the reference exists the carry to 10 MPa is off by 1.5 to 5 % where a direct evaluation is within 0.4 %. The plan's P3 bullet "replace or restrict the fixed-compressibility liquid approximation" must become: evaluate liquids directly at the state pressure with the same EOS, before any pressure-domain extension. `TranslatedPengRobinson` already exposes everything needed; the change is in `HydrocarbonModel` and in the liquid node Jacobian that chains the correction. |
| F2 | must | **The degree-five Cp fits cannot reach the steam-cracking reference.** Measured against NIST JANAF Shomate values (methane, nitrogen) and the Gurvich table (ethane): methane −0.7 % at 1000 K, −2.4 % at 1073 K, −5.9 % at 1143 K, −10.8 % at 1200 K, −26 % at 1300 K; ethane −1.1 % at 1100 K, −3.7 % at 1200 K; nitrogen +1.0 % at 1073 K, +2.6 % at 1143 K, +5.0 % at 1200 K. The fits are excellent inside 298 to 900 K (within 0.1 %). The plan's instruction not to extrapolate them is right; the record type needs a form with a physical high-temperature limit (NASA 9- or 7-coefficient two-range polynomials, section 5), and the loader must refuse evaluation outside a fit's declared range rather than rely on the package envelope. |
| F3 | must | **The high-temperature target is an ideal-gas problem, not an EOS problem.** At 1073 K / 2 bar and 1143 K / 2.5 bar the residual enthalpy of CH4, C2H6, N2 and H2O is 0.002 to 0.06 % of the enthalpy change from 298 K, and Z is within 0.08 % of 1. Conversely, CoolProp's own ideal-gas part for methane (Setzmann and Wagner 1991, fitted to 625 K) is +1.7 % off JANAF at 1100 K, and its Tmax for methane, ethane, ethylene and propane is 625, 675, 450 and 650 K. So neither CoolProp nor a Helmholtz port can serve as the oracle above about 650 K for the light hydrocarbons; JANAF/CEA is the reference there, and the "reference Helmholtz backend" option does not address the plan's high-temperature goal at all. |
| F4 | should | **The cubic's accuracy envelope is now measured (section 3) and should replace P1's open comparison.** Declared-error grade is reachable everywhere the plan targets except: the critical neighbourhood (Tr 0.95 to 1.1 with Pr 0.8 to 1.5, where density errors reach −15 % and cp errors tens of %), liquid cp above Tr about 0.85 (+12 % N2 at 110 K, +16 % CH4 at 170 K), and ethane's saturation pressure below 150 K (+10 % at 120 K, +29 % at 90 K, the known low-Tr weakness of the Soave alpha; the Twu alpha fixes exactly this). The plan should carry these as explicit exclusion bands and as the measured argument for or against the Twu alpha, instead of "a consistent dense-fluid model" as an unspecified P1 outcome. |
| F5 | should | **No mixture stability test exists, and the plan does not name one.** Section 4 of the plan requires "actual mixture stability" for supercritical classification and phase appearance. The code has `TranslatedPengRobinson.vaporBranch` (an algebraic root heuristic), the V3 flash assumes two phases, and the fluid network's `PhaseLayout` carries fixed liquid/vapour/water slots. P3 must name a tangent-plane stability test (Michelsen) on the existing analytic d ln phi/dn and budget its cost; without it "phase appearance/disappearance and near-critical phase merging" in P3 cannot be tested. |
| F6 | should | **Pilot solid species.** The plan's P4 (CO2 in N2 deposition) is well chosen; the P5 methane/ethane liquidus is not the easiest first SLE case. Ethane has a solid II to I transition at 89.81 K, 0.55 K below its triple point (90.34 K), with 2.28 kJ/mol against a fusion enthalpy of 0.58 kJ/mol (Atake and Chihara 1976), so a single-crystal ethane model is wrong at the liquidus by construction. The CH4/C2H6 eutectic (73.88 K at x_CH4 = 0.680, Liu et al. 2022) lies below both pure Tmin values. Recommendation: CO2 freezing out of liquid methane (Davis et al. 1962; Zhang et al. 2011; Le and Trebble 2007; the Maltby et al. 2025 review) as the P5 case, with the same solid CO2 Gibbs model as P4; methane/ethane stays research-only until a two-phase ethane solid exists. This keeps the four-compound pilot unchanged. |
| F7 | should | **Data spine is missing from P2.** The plan wants shared per-species enthalpy/entropy references across phases and, later, reaction-enthalpy support. The mechanism is: h(T,P,x) = sum x_i [ΔfH°_i(298.15) + h_ig,i(T) − h_ig,i(298.15)] + h_res(T,P,x), with s built the same way from S°_i(298.15, 1 bar), and every evaluator (cubic, Helmholtz oracle, solid Gibbs functions) using the same ideal-gas polynomials and formation values. CoolProp's fluid files already carry ATcT 1.220 formation enthalpies with uncertainties (CO2 −393477 ± 15 J/mol, CH4 −74513 ± 43 J/mol); CEA `thermo.inp` carries S°298 and the polynomials. With this spine a solid reference is anchored at the triple point to the fluid model's chemical potential, which gives triple-point consistency by construction rather than by a test. |
| F8 | should | **"One canonical revision per component across units" needs a precise rule.** The engine in section 4 of the plan supports several evaluators; the rule should be "one parameter set per (component, evaluator family); a package selects one evaluator family; cross-package consistency is guaranteed by the shared ideal-gas spine, and the residual difference between families is measured and published". Otherwise the rule forbids the very hybrid the plan allows. |
| F9 | should | **NeqSim can be demoted now.** Apache-2.0, Java 17, version 3.22.0 (2026-09-23); the only published artifact is a 60 MB shaded jar with no package relocation (bundles log4j-core, gson, commons-lang3, jackson, xstream, h2, jfreechart); the component database is `COMP.csv` loaded into an in-memory H2 database with `CSVREAD('file:'+url)`; September 2026 issues report three Antoine defaults covering 63 % of rows, silent NaN/Infinity returns, and derivative stubs returning 0. Its solid stack (`PhaseSolid`, `FreezingPointTemperatureFlash`, `SolidFlash`, Michelsen stability) is useful as a second offline oracle for solid flashes, run from a tool harness with 8 to 10 threads at most and never in the mod. P1 option 2 should be closed with this evidence rather than tested for integration. |
| F10 | should | **Process anchors: add the pressure bases the research returned, and treat the plan's [R4] 36 bar as compressor discharge, not furnace pressure.** Steam-cracking coil pressure 170 to 250 kPa (Zimmermann and Walzl, via the 2021 Energies review), dilution steam 0.3 kg/kg ethane and about 0.5 kg/kg naphtha, residence 0.1 to 0.5 s, coil outlet 819 to 884 °C, transfer-line exchanger outlet 576 to 592 °C (US 5,990,370); delayed coker heater outlet 769 to 783 K at 40 to 60 psig, drum 20 to 30 psig (2.4 to 3.1 bar absolute); VGO hydrotreating 573 to 713 K with 30 to 100 barg hydrogen partial pressure; vacuum flash zone 392 to 422 °C at 30 to 40 mmHg (4.0 to 5.3 kPa), against Case A's 13.1 kPa; air-separation high-pressure column 5 to 6 bar and low-pressure column 1.2 to 1.3 bar absolute. Several are patent or vendor values (section 7); P0 should confirm from Ullmann's or a textbook before freezing numbers. |
| F11 | should | **Hydrogen.** PR78 density for H2 at 623 to 700 K and 6 to 10 MPa is within 0.6 % of Leachman et al. 2009 (Z about 1.02 to 1.03), and the Soave alpha is monotonic for H2 up to Tr about 1270, so the "high-pressure hydrogen EOS" part of the hydrotreating target is already met. The risk is entirely in H2/hydrocarbon interaction parameters and hydrogen solubility in heavy cuts. E-PPR78 has an H2 group; the validation data are Lin, Sebastian and Chao 1980 (H2 + n-hexadecane, 463 to 664 K, 2 to 25 MPa, the only open set inside the hydrotreating window), the Mohammadi et al. 2021 compilation (919 points, 26 hydrocarbons, 213 to 623 K, 0.1 to 25.5 MPa, open access) and the augmented Grayson-Streed work of Torres, de Hemptinne and Machin 2013 for heavy cuts. |
| F12 | note | **The VDU bias is already documented and should be cited, not rediscovered.** `documentation/2026-08-31-v3-vdu/V3_VDU_LITERATURE_AND_THERMO_DATA.md` section 2.4 measures PR78 4 to 13 K colder than Maxwell-Bonnell for the VGO cuts at 1 to 13 kPa and proposes a two-point anchoring of omega and Tc per cut. P0's "freeze process-driven targets" for VDU is that decision. |
| F13 | note | **Water at high temperature.** IAPWS-IF97 region 2 (to 1073.15 K, 100 MPa) and region 5 (1073.15 to 2273.15 K, 50 MPa) are free with attribution and cover the steam-cracking coil and quench; `WaterRegion1` already exists. The current vapour path (Shomate plus Watson latent heat) should be checked against region 2 before it is relied on above 900 K. The plan's deferral of full aqueous work stays valid. |
| F14 | note | **Provenance of existing records under the owner's non-commercial statement.** The nitrogen record is a fit to NIST WebBook Shomate and zero-pressure Cp values, the viscosity tables are sampled from NIST WebBook fluid data, and the liquid calibration points are NIST values; Burcat's ideal-gas database is licensed for non-commercial use only. The owner states the project is non-commercial, so none of this blocks; the records should keep their `source`/`provenance` fields as they do now. Where two sources are equal, the cleaner one (CoolProp MIT, CEA Apache-2.0, IAPWS attribution, NIST AGA8 public-domain notice) is preferred. |
| F15 | note | **Transport.** CoolProp's MIT fluid files carry viscosity and conductivity models for every pilot species with stated ranges (N2/O2/Ar Lemmon and Jacobsen 2004; CO2 Laesecke and Muzny 2017 with Huber et al. 2016 conductivity to 1100 K; CH4 Sotiriadou et al. 2025 to 625 K; C2H6 Friend et al. 1991; H2 Muzny et al. 2013 and Assael et al. 2011 to 1000 K; NH3 Monogenidou et al. 2018 to 725 K). Some are "hardcoded" in the C++ (methane conductivity, water) and would be ported from the MIT source. Ethylene and H2S have no conductivity model and CO none at all. The existing log-table approach remains valid for the network; ranges must be declared per table as the plan says. |

## 4. Route comparison and recommendation

| Criterion | A. Extend the in-house cubic | B. Helmholtz port (CoolProp pure-fluid coefficients + GERG-2008 mixture rules) | C. NeqSim at runtime | D. CoolProp/REFPROP at runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy at pilot states | Declared-error grade (section 3); excluded critical band | Reference grade within each EOS range (density 0.02 to 0.1 %) | Same cubics as A, or its GERG port | Reference grade |
| Reach to 1143 K (light HC) | Yes, with CEA/JANAF Cp0 (residual < 0.1 %) | No: CH4 625 K, C2H6 675 K, C2H4 450 K; ideal part off by 1.7 % at 1100 K | No | No |
| Reach to 10 MPa | Yes after F1 | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Crude cuts, coking, VDU | Yes (the existing packages) | No: no pseudo-component Helmholtz model exists | Cubic only | No |
| Mixture parameters | E-PPR78 group kij(T), 40 groups incl. N2, CO2, H2S, H2, O2, Ar, CO, He, water; pseudo-cuts via Xu et al. 2015 | GERG-2008 pairs for the 21 natural-gas components (no NH3, no pseudo-cuts) | Its own tables (quality issues) | Same as B |
| Solids consistency | Pure-solid Gibbs functions anchored to the cubic's liquid chemical potential at the triple point | Same, anchored to the Helmholtz fluid | Sub-cooled-liquid reference with Hfus, Cp corrections (undocumented) | None |
| Derivatives, solver fit | Analytic d ln phi/dn, dT, dP already in `PengRobinsonKernel` and `TranslatedPengRobinson`; both solvers run on it | New derivative code; density iteration per evaluation (10 to 50x the cubic's cost) | Object-heavy, finite-difference dfugdt | Native library, excluded by the no-native rule |
| Runtime and determinism | Unchanged | Measurable but unmeasured | Fat jar, H2 database, silent NaN returns | Excluded |
| Licence | Existing code | MIT coefficients; NIST public-domain notice for AGA8 | Apache-2.0 | MIT (CoolProp), REFPROP commercial |
| Effort | Medium: F1, Cp0 records, kij, stability test, solids | High: term families, mixture rules, derivatives, stability | High: trimmed relocated build, weekly upstream | n/a |

**Recommendation: route A for production, with a Java port of the pure-fluid Helmholtz evaluator built first as an in-JVM test oracle (not a runtime evaluator).** Reasons: the measured cubic accuracy already meets the plan's declared-error state outside the critical band; every process family the plan names is cubic territory (pseudo-cuts, hydrogen, near-ideal high-temperature gas); the analytic Jacobians and both solvers exist for it; a Helmholtz backend would not extend the high-temperature validity and adds cost only where the pilot needs least. The oracle port (CoolProp's `ResidualHelmholtzPower`, `Gaussian`, `NonAnalytic` for water and CO2, `GaoB` for ammonia, and the alpha0 families; about one to two thousand lines) removes Python from the gate suite, makes reference values deterministic and MIT-clean, and leaves the door open: if G3 shows a consumer that needs reference-grade cryogenic or near-critical accuracy, the same code becomes the light-gas package evaluator and the mixture rules are the next step, with GERG-2008 pairs from CoolProp's `dev/mixtures` or NIST's `GERG2008.cpp`.

What "extend the cubic" concretely means for P3:

1. Liquids evaluated at the state pressure; delete the 2 MPa reference and `GlobalLiquidResponse` from the network path (F1). Keep the constant translation; anchor it, as tc-PR does, at the saturated-liquid density at Tr = 0.8 from the reference EOS (CoolProp) instead of ad-hoc points, so that every component is anchored the same way.
2. Alpha function: keep the Soave alpha for the pilot because the E-PPR78 kij were regressed with it, and it is monotonic in every target window (spurious minimum at Tr = (1 + 1/m)²: H2 about 1270, CH4 12.6, C2H6 8.4, water 4.6, a heavy cut with omega 1.2 about 2.3, all beyond the process maxima). Measure the consistent Twu alpha (Le Guennec et al. 2016; parameters in Pina-Martinez et al. 2018 supporting information) as a P3 option against the ethane low-Tr Psat error and the liquid cp error; adopting it requires revalidating the kij (no group-contribution kij exists for tc-PR).
3. Ideal gas: a `nasa9` (or `nasa7` two-range) record type from CEA `thermo.inp`, evaluated only inside its declared ranges; pseudo-cuts keep their fits bounded at 900 K, or are refitted with Laštovka and Shaw 2013 (to 1500 K, needs an elemental composition estimate from Tb and specific gravity) when the coking/cracking cases need it. The shared reference convention of F7 goes into P2.
4. Mixing: E-PPR78 kij(T) for the real species and Xu et al. 2015 for pseudo-cuts; "zero" stays an explicit estimate with a label, as the plan says. Validate H2 pairs on Lin et al. 1980 and Mohammadi et al. 2021.
5. A Michelsen tangent-plane stability test on the existing derivative bundle, used for phase count, supercritical classification and phase birth; budget it (F5).
6. Solids as pure-crystal Gibbs functions g_s(T,P) = g_l(T_t,P_t) + [g_s(T,P) − g_s(T_t,P_t)] built from solid Cp(T), a solid volume with its expansion/compressibility, and (for ethane later) the solid-solid transition; anchored to the fluid model at the triple point so melting, sublimation and the triple point are consistent by construction. Sources per species in section 5. Onset = first chemical-potential crossing; finite inventories through the conserved species matrix, as the plan states.
7. Critical neighbourhood: a declared exclusion band (Tr 0.95 to 1.1 and Pr 0.8 to 1.5 for each pure fluid; the mixture envelope's critical region once the stability test exists) reported as research-only, not as a fabricated number.

## 7. Open decisions for the owner

1. Accept declared-error grade (cubic) for the pilot's cryogenic and dense states, with the critical band excluded, or require reference grade in the runtime for the light-gas package (route B for that package, at the measured extra cost). Recommended: declared-error grade now, oracle port first.
2. Pilot solid species: CO2-only solids in the pilot (recommended) or the plan's methane/ethane SLE.
3. Alpha function: Soave (E-PPR78 compatible) for the pilot with Twu measured as an option (recommended), or Twu from the start with own kij validation.
4. Whether the Helmholtz oracle port is done in Java tests (recommended: removes Python from the gates) or CoolProp stays a Python-side tool.
5. The VDU K-value policy already open in the VDU batch (PR78 as characterized versus a two-point Maxwell-Bonnell anchoring).
