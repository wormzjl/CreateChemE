## 1. Objective and agreed direction

Build one thermodynamic service for CreateChemE that supplies mutually consistent phase properties, equilibrium and energy/volume closure to the fluid network and, where supported, column/process consumers. Combine gas, liquid and qualified solid behavior, including supercritical fluids, behind shared data and evaluation contracts.

**A major goal is to extend the scientifically qualified temperature and pressure validity of the current property packages and their consumers.** The work must demonstrate useful operating ranges beyond current limits, including lower-temperature fluid behavior and pressures needed for dense/supercritical fluids, while investigating higher-temperature and lower-pressure extensions where justified. This is a model, data and solver qualification objective, not permission to widen JSON limits. New compounds or a new API alone do not satisfy it. Hydrocarbon qualification must target CDU/VDU and downstream coking/hydrotreating conditions; for lighter compounds, steam-cracking conditions are an explicit high-temperature reference. These process targets guide both the pilot evidence and subsequent package rollout.

**Build and validate the engine with a few representative compounds first. Broad compound additions come only after the pilot engine and its runtime integration pass.** Initial pilot candidates are existing nitrogen, methane and ethane, plus CO2 as one targeted new compound. This four-compound set exercises cryogenic fluids, supercritical behavior and, through CO2 alone (D2), sublimation/deposition in a carrier gas and freezing out of a liquid mixture. Methane and ethane are pilot fluids for the extended T/P range, the liquid solvent of the CO2 freezing case and the light-species steam-cracking reference; their own liquidus is a research-only case (ethane has a solid-solid transition 0.55 K below its triple point with four times its fusion enthalpy, and the eutectic at 73.88 K lies below both pure triple points). P0/P1 may substitute a better-supported pilot within a similarly small set, with a documented reason. Existing water may be used as a comparison without making full ice/aqueous integration another pilot prerequisite.

After that pilot, add or extend the required chemical identities and qualified property datasets for air components and ammonia in staged database releases. CO2 enters earlier only because it serves the pilot. Solid-gas transitions remain limited to selected compounds and domains with adequate data. Database membership does not imply that every phase transition or every mixture containing that compound is supported.

Research may change backend, equations, ordering within the pilot, operating envelopes, enabled solids and release scope. The engine-first prerequisite for broad database expansion remains in force. Other stable requirements are conservation, reference consistency, explicit scientific coverage, engine scheduling rules and truthful unavailable-state handling.

## 3. Capability and domain contract

Maintain an explicit matrix by component, property package, phase combination, composition region and T/P domain. Suggested states are qualified, estimated-with-declared-error, research-only, unavailable and out-of-domain. Exact schema names remain open until P2.

Track at least:

- Gas/liquid/supercritical properties and caloric/mechanical derivatives.
- Vapor-liquid equilibrium and, where needed, liquid-liquid equilibrium.
- Solid-liquid and solid-gas equilibrium for explicitly identified crystal phases.
- Solid density, heat capacity and reference energy/entropy, independently from a measured boundary temperature.
- Transport properties with their own validity; a valid thermodynamic state does not imply valid viscosity or conductivity.
- Mixture interactions and applicable composition ranges. A pure-fluid dataset does not validate all mixtures containing that species.

A melting or sublimation curve can qualify an onset diagnostic without qualifying finite solid amounts or latent-energy simulation. Mark that distinction. Gas-solid support may initially include only CO2 and/or water, depending on the data audit; ammonia, air gases and hydrocarbons are admitted individually after evidence exists.

Unavailable is not “never freezes/deposits.” When a requested equilibrium path needs unqualified phases or data, return a clear unsupported/domain result. Runtime behavior should use the existing held-state/error mechanism, retain owned inventory, and publish through the engine schedule. An explicitly constrained fluid-only calculation may remain available for a declared purpose, but must not be reported as fully stable equilibrium when solid stability is unknown.

Qualified transition capabilities are derived from consistent phase models. Do not create independent switches that permit mutually contradictory phase equilibria. At a state involving vapor, liquid and solid competition, the chosen package must be qualified for that competition or refuse it.

### Current-package T/P expansion deliverable

P0 must record actual package and component limits, model/correlation limits, solver constraints, transport limits and consumer restrictions separately. The inspected methane/ethane fluid records start at 293.15 K; nitrogen starts at 63.151 K; the selected network has a 2 MPa pressure ceiling. Column and network domains can differ. These are baselines to improve and qualify, not permanent bounds of the new engine.

Two measured defects of the current formulation are prerequisites of any domain extension (review F1 and F2). The network's liquid path evaluates every liquid at a fixed 2 MPa reference and carries it to the state pressure with a global compressibility; it has no liquid reference state wherever the saturation pressure exceeds 2 MPa (nitrogen above about 115 K, methane 165 K, ethane 270 K, CO2 253 K), with measured density errors of -20 % to -95 %, and the global 1e-9 /Pa is 2 to 9 times smaller than the real compressibility of the cryogenic liquids. Liquids must be evaluated at the state pressure with the same equation of state before the pressure ceiling moves. The degree-five ideal-gas Cp fits are within 0.12 % over 298 to 900 K and diverge above it (methane -5.9 % at 1143 K and -10.8 % at 1200 K; ethane -3.7 % at 1200 K; nitrogen +2.6 % at 1143 K); the loader must refuse evaluation outside a fit's declared range, and the high-temperature extension uses bounded polynomial records (P2). The critical neighbourhood of each pure fluid (about Tr 0.95 to 1.1 with Pr 0.8 to 1.5, where the cubic's density error reaches -15 % and its heat capacity is unusable) is a declared research-only band, not a fabricated number. The P1 study of 2026-09-24 (`P1_ALPHA_AND_LIQUID_PATH_STUDY.md`) found two neighbouring regions the band misses: liquid at Tr 0.90 to 0.95 with Pr 0.58 to 0.74 (density to -9.3 %, cp to +30 %) and dense supercritical fluid at Tr 1.05 to 1.2 with Pr 2 to 3 (density to -7 %); P2 either widens the band to include them or gives each its own declared error, and the acceptance targets of P0 carry those numbers.

For the pilot, publish a before/target/achieved T/P coverage table for each affected current package and active composition/phase family. Include continuous paths from the existing range into the extension, accuracy/conservation results and any remaining exclusions. Test both interior states and each newly admitted boundary, plus just-outside-domain refusal. Retain the current supported region unless an explicit scientific defect requires a separately documented correction.

Extending an existing package is not a claim that every component in it is valid throughout a rectangular global envelope. Existing crude cuts, inactive species, water treatment, mixture composition and phase-specific limitations must be resolved honestly. Qualified pilot component domains and appropriate package/state guards may widen useful supported states without pretending that all crude mixtures are cryogenically valid. A detached demonstration package alone is insufficient: P6 must prove the widened qualified behavior is usable through the applicable current network/process package paths.

### Process-driven hydrocarbon T/P requirements

The following are preliminary research anchors, not a universally approved rectangular package range. P0 must replace them with specific process cases, pressure bases, feed/composition definitions, margins and acceptance targets. Use bulk process-fluid temperatures, not furnace-flame or tube-metal temperatures. Store pressures as absolute SI values; preserve source gauge/absolute labels and conversions. Pilot evidence probes the engine and existing package paths; complete process-mixture coverage follows in P7.

| Process family | Initial anchor for research | Required thermodynamic coverage |
|---|---|---|
| CDU | Atmospheric/pressurized crude fractionation, hot flash zone and cooler overhead/side draws; freeze numeric cases from the existing qualified literature-CDU batch in P0 | Shared crude-cut VLE, density, Cp, enthalpy, steam/water treatment and phase splits across the whole unit; preserve current qualified cases |
| VDU | Existing local Case A specifies a 382 degC (655.15 K) flash zone at 13.10 kPa absolute, with an assumed approximately 10.6 kPa top; broader deep-vacuum envelope to be justified separately [R1] | Valid low-pressure saturation/VLE, caloric properties, vacuum density and steam interactions; physical accuracy across intermediate pressures back to CDU |
| Coking | Representative delayed-coker feed entering the drum at 870-910 degF (approximately 466-488 degC / 739-761 K), as described by OSHA/EPA [R2]; select actual drum/heater pressures and paths in P0 | Heavy-feed and selected product properties at hot process conditions, vapor/liquid behavior and energy references; coke formation/yield is chemical conversion, not freezing |
| Hydrotreating | Shell's diesel example: 350-390 degC (623-663 K), 60-90 barg, approximately 6.1-9.1 MPa absolute using nominal atmospheric pressure [R3] | High-pressure hydrogen/hydrocarbon mixture properties, hydrogen solubility and gas/liquid partitioning, enthalpy and hot/cold separator paths; heavier-feed severity requires separate cases |
| Steam cracking, especially light compounds | Linde cites 800-870 degC (1073.15-1143.15 K), with steam dilution [R4]; obtain explicit coil pressure and composition cases in P0 | High-temperature species Cp/h/u/s, gas-mixture and transport properties through heating and quench; composition-dependent reactor thermodynamics without pretending the feed is chemically stable indefinitely |
| Steam-cracker downstream train | Quench/cooling, compression and cold separation; Linde cites approximately 36 bar after compression without specifying pressure basis on that overview [R4] | Use a confirmed absolute pressure basis before numerical qualification; cover changing steam/water and hydrocarbon phases across the train, not only the furnace outlet |

The 900 K ceiling is therefore insufficient for the light-compound target. P0 must qualify a high-temperature endpoint reaching at least the selected steam-cracking case (nominally up to about 1143 K), and assess a candidate 1200 K model ceiling as an explicitly provisional margin. The 2 MPa ceiling is insufficient for the cited diesel hydrotreating case; assess coverage through its approximately 9.1 MPa absolute upper reference and a provisional 10 MPa qualification ceiling. Neither provisional margin is already validated, and more severe hydrotreating or another process may require a different ceiling. Do not extend all pseudocomponents or all phases to those bounds merely because one species passes.

Use one canonical property/interaction revision for the same real component or identically defined pseudo-cut across CDU, VDU and downstream units. Unit names must not select separate atmospheric-, vacuum-, coker- or hydrotreater-tuned values. Fit and validate jointly across the intended regimes, including intermediate states and energy continuity on inter-unit transfer. This follows the existing shared CDU/VDU contract [R1]. Distinct assays or materially refined pseudo-cuts may need distinct identities and an explicit conservative material/enthalpy mapping; a unit handoff alone is not a reason to retune a component.

Reaction boundary: these are required property-domain/application references, not a claim that a phase-equilibrium engine predicts coking, hydrotreating or steam-cracking yields. Qualify properties for specified species/compositions, including a declared frozen-composition gas state where appropriate, while identifying where decomposition/reaction makes an unrestricted equilibrium claim invalid. Reaction kinetics, species conversion and coke chemistry remain a separate implementation scope, but the thermodynamic reference/data design must support later reaction enthalpies consistently. Do not represent petroleum coke as a frozen crude cut or extrapolate a heavy-feed fit through chemical conversion and call it a validated product stream.

## 4. Proposed architecture, subject to P1 research

Material catalog -> phase evaluators -> phase stability/equilibrium -> phase-resolved state and derivatives -> device/process solvers.

- Versioned chemical identity, common conserved component basis, reference conventions, model parameters, provenance, uncertainty and validity.
- Phase evaluators for fluid EOS/activity models and selected solid models. Provide chemical potentials, h/u/s/v and required derivatives from a consistent formulation.
- One equilibrium service with TP, PH and UV use cases and access to direct phase evaluations for coupled device residuals. Avoid a mandatory full nested flash in every network Newton trial.
- Results containing a list of phases, chemical compositions, amounts, energies/volumes, classification and coverage evidence. Support multiple liquids/solids without forcing every package to implement all of them.
- Supercritical is a fluid regime. No separately conserved supercritical inventory, artificial latent heat or fixed liquid/vapor switch in that region. Use actual mixture stability, not weighted critical constants.
- Keep inert particles distinct from chemically transforming solids. A chemical solid amount need not imply a suspended particle population or a wall deposit.
- Transport and finite-rate models share data/state interfaces but remain separate physics. Equilibrium does not predict nucleation delay, particle size, deposit porosity or blockage rate.

At fixed T/P, use Gibbs stability/equilibrium; at fixed U/V, enforce energy/volume and the appropriate equilibrium conditions. Retain nonnegative amounts and conservation. Report underdetermined pure TP coexistence rather than inventing a phase fraction. Do not promise finite smooth derivatives exactly at critical singularities.

Use shared per-species enthalpy/entropy references across phases. Validate liquid/vapor/solid chemical-potential and latent-heat consistency, including the triple point. A common reference offset alone does not establish agreement of the derivatives or phase boundaries.

The shared reference is a data spine (D1): h(T,P,x) = sum over i of x_i [dHf_i(298.15 K) + h_ig,i(T) - h_ig,i(298.15 K)] + h_res(T,P,x), and s likewise from S_i(298.15 K, 1 bar), with one set of ideal-gas polynomials and formation values under every evaluator (cubic, Helmholtz oracle, solid Gibbs functions). A pure solid is anchored at the triple point to the fluid model's chemical potential, so melting, sublimation and the triple point agree by construction. The parameter rule is one parameter set per (component, evaluator family); a package selects one evaluator family; the residual difference between families is measured and published. Phase count, supercritical classification and phase birth come from a tangent-plane stability test on the evaluators' analytic composition derivatives, not from a root heuristic.
